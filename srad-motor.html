PROJECT // SRAD SOLID ROCKET MOTOR

SRAD H-Class Solid Rocket Motor

38mm RNX-71 — Design Through Manufacturing

CLASS 38mm H-Class / 400 psi
NOZZLE 2-Part Al / Phenolic, Conical
ANALYSIS MATLAB / ANSYS / RPA
STATUS Paused — Resuming Independently
Propulsion SRAD MATLAB ANSYS Nozzle Design

Overview

This was Liberty Rocketry's first attempt at a fully student-researched-and-developed motor. I led the project end-to-end — design, analysis, and the start of manufacturing — before it was paused by the university for review. Development continues independently.

Why RNX-71

As a first SRAD engine, minimizing risk while proving out the full design-to-manufacture pipeline mattered more than performance. RNX-71 is inexpensive, low-power, and low-consequence, making it well suited for validating process and procedure — including navigating the university's safety approval — before scaling up.

Design Process

Starting from the target scope (38mm, H-class, 400 psi chamber pressure), I worked outward: sized the throat and propellant grain using hand calculations and MATLAB, solved case and closure geometry to close out the pressure vessel design, ran thermal and fluid interaction checks by hand and then in ANSYS, and cross-validated overall motor performance in OpenRocket and RPA before settling on a final design. I modeled the design in CAD and 3D-printed a scale mockup to verify fit before committing to metal.

Nozzle Design

Designed a two-part nozzle: an aluminum carrier with a phenolic insert, chosen specifically for conical geometry so it could be manufactured on a manual lathe — a manufacturing-constraint-driven decision rather than a performance-optimal one, which mattered for a first-time in-house build.

Manufacturing & Process Planning

While the case and closure hardware was on order, I finalized the propellant mixing and casting process, including batch ratios and quality-control checks. The manufacturing plan called for 4 grains — 3 for the motor, 1 dedicated to quality testing. Case and closure manufacturing began once material arrived, before the project was paused.

Status

Design is complete and validated across multiple tools. Manufacturing is paused pending university review and will resume independently.
