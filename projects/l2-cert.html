L2 Certification Rocket

Dual-Motor Cert Build — Design, Failure Analysis, and Rebuild

High-Power Rocketry Recovery Systems Failure Analysis PCB Design
Motors H550 (L1) / J425 (L2)
Recovery Dual-Deploy, Chute Release
Stability 11.9% (L1) / 9% (L2)
Status Rebuilding — Adding Flight Computer

Overview

Built and flew a minimal-diameter Blue Tube airframe rocket to certify both Level 1 and Level 2 with Tripoli, clearing the way to mix propellant and assemble the team's M2500 competition motor. The build used a 36" main parachute on a chute-release and ripcord dual-deploy setup, with no onboard avionics.

Build

The kit came minimally prepped. General assembly used 5-minute epoxy throughout, with aerospace-grade epoxy specifically for the fin joints given the higher structural loads on a J-class flight.

Certification Flights

Level 1 — H550: Flown to a planned 491m. Stability margin of 11.9%. Flight, deployment, and recovery all performed cleanly.

Level 2 — J425: Flown to a planned 1013m. Stability margin of 9%. The rocket coned near apogee — most likely a combination of reduced stability margin at low velocity and wind gusts at the top of the flight — and then suffered a deployment failure: the upper and lower airframes fully separated when a recovery knot came undone, compounded by zippering along the ripcord path. With no onboard electronics and a difficult recovery area, the rocket wasn't recovered until a week later, by which point exposure to rain had warped the aft airframe.

Root Cause Analysis

The failure traced to two compounding issues rather than a motor or stability problem alone: an insecure recovery knot that allowed full separation, and ripcord zippering that likely followed the initial separation event. The lower stability margin on the L2 flight (9% vs. 11.9% on L1) plausibly contributed to the coning that preceded deployment, alongside wind conditions at apogee.

Current Rebuild

The aft airframe section is being replaced, with an additional coupler added lower in the airframe for structural margin against a repeat separation. I'm also designing a low-cost (~$20) custom flight computer PCB — both to get real deployment and flight data on the re-flight, and as a hands-on entry point into electronics design. Targeting a re-flight within the next six weeks.
